For people who run coding agents

See your agents without leaving what you’re doing.

The AI usage dashboard reads what Claude Code and Codex already write on your Mac. Session titles, contents and your project names are never sent; token, cost and plan-limit figures are part of the usage data Oriel sends, as the Privacy Policy you accept in the app describes. The orb beside the closed notch turns while an agent works, and a question from an agent is announced there until you answer.

  1. Limits. Five-hour and weekly windows as a double ring each, amber from 90%. Claude’s come from the Claude app once you switch that on, or live from Anthropic with the sign-in Claude Code saved; Codex’s from its own transcripts while a Codex task runs here, or live from chatgpt.com. Both live readings are off until you switch them on.
  2. API value. What today’s, the week’s or the month’s tokens would cost at list API prices (not what your plan costs), with each model’s share and how much came from cache.
  3. Working now. Up to three live sessions, anything waiting on your answer first. Click a row to open that chat in its app.
The fine print, up front
  • Reading what is inside session files is off until you choose it (“Read labels from session files”); until then it sees only the names, dates and sizes of the transcript files.
  • Token counts live in memory unless you also switch on “Save usage history on this Mac”, which is off by default and deletable. Their daily totals by model are part of the usage data Oriel sends.
  • Permission prompts leave nothing in a transcript, so Oriel can’t see them. A pending Codex approval is invisible too.
  • The dashboard’s daily price list is the one download it makes by default; it can be switched off.

A tour of the notch

Invisible, until something happens.

Closed, Oriel sits exactly inside your physical notch. When something starts, it widens into a live activity beside the camera housing. Scroll, and follow it.

Open

One nook, several rooms.

Hover or click and it opens onto three pages: Dashboard, Tray and Tools. Pick a room below; each picture is Oriel itself, not a mock-up.

Dashboard

Pin the widgets you want side by side: media, calendar, timers, stats, Caffeinate and more. If the panel is too narrow for all of them, the rest wait in a More tray, still one click away.

  • Now Playing from Music and Spotify, with scrubbing and transport
  • Today’s events and the week ahead, read on this Mac only
  • Arranged by dragging, in Settings

Tray

A shelf for files in transit. Drag something in, move on, drag it back out when you’re ready. Files stay where they are; the Tray only points at them.

  • Quick Look and multi-select
  • Persists across launches

Tools

Notes and To-Do that autosave locally, your macOS Shortcuts to run with a click, Stats, quick apps and the optional online widgets. Anything that needs more room than a Dashboard column.

  • Notes and To-Do: local, searchable, autosaving
  • Shortcuts: lists and runs yours; pin the ones you use

Calendar

Today’s events and the week ahead through macOS’s own calendar access, plus a month grid with start and end times. Reminders can join the list, but only if you switch them on, and they are never added, edited or completed.

Timers

A countdown, a stopwatch and a pomodoro, each running on its own. The closed notch shows whichever ends soonest, named for what it is, and says what finished when it’s done.

Devices

The Mac’s battery and, once you ask for them, your accessories’: keyboard, mouse, trackpad and earbuds with their case. No Bluetooth permission needed; accessories are read only while the widget is on screen.

AI sessions

A page of its own for Claude Code and Codex: plan limits, what your tokens would cost at list prices, and which sessions are working or waiting on you. The spotlight is just above.

Local-first, and checkable

It works for you. And you can check it.

  • No account. You don’t sign in to use Oriel.
  • Usage data, in the terms. This version sends usage data to Oriel’s own collector, as the License Agreement and Privacy Policy you accept in the app describe. It covers which features you use and your settings, the symbols, leagues and teams you follow, the town or city and country Weather shows, AI usage figures (tokens, cost and plan limits) and summaries of Oriel’s network requests; never keys, sign-ins, what you type, songs or your exact location. Delete what it sent in Settings ▸ Privacy.
  • No licence server. The app doesn’t check a licence anywhere to run.

Oriel’s own network client reaches only the hosts in the list below, each behind its own switch except Oriel’s own collector, which is part of the terms and has none. Two things go outside that client: the chat sheet’s web pages (claude.ai or chatgpt.com, and the sign-in pages they send you to) and Apple’s place service for Weather. The client refuses every other address in code, and Settings ▸ Privacy shows how many requests it has made to each host since launch, and how the last one was answered.

Where it lives
The notch and the menu bar. No Dock icon.
  1. Get the app. Beta 0.1.5 is open to invited testers in the United States. Its release page on GitHub has the disk image, its SHA-256 checksum and the install steps. It sends usage data, as its terms describe.
  2. Move it to Applications. Needed for “Open at login” to be offered.
  3. Open it. First it asks you to accept its License Agreement and Privacy Policy. A four-page Setup Assistant (Welcome, Features, Permissions, All set) lets you tick what you want. Nothing you choose in it is saved until you press Open Oriel.
  4. Allow things only when a feature asks. The assistant only shows permission states; each feature asks for its own, at first use.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do I need a Mac with a notch?

The point of Oriel is the notch, but on a display with no camera housing it draws its panel at the top centre of the screen.

Which Macs and macOS versions?

Apple Silicon Macs on macOS 15 or later. It’s built and tested on macOS 27; some effects, like Liquid Glass, need macOS 26 and fall back gracefully on older versions.

Does it send my data anywhere?

Usage data, yes: this version sends it to Oriel’s own collector, as the License Agreement and Privacy Policy you accept in the app describe. It covers which features you use and your settings, the symbols, leagues and teams you follow, the town or city and country Weather shows, AI usage figures (tokens, cost and plan limits) and summaries of Oriel’s network requests; it never includes keys, sign-ins, what you type, songs or your exact location. It is part of the terms you accept, with no separate switch, and you can delete what your Mac has sent in Settings ▸ Privacy. There’s no account. Everything else that Oriel’s network client reaches goes only to the other named hosts in the table above, every one behind its own switch; the chat sheet’s web pages and Apple’s place service for Weather go outside that client, and Settings ▸ Privacy shows how many requests it has made to each host since launch, and how the last one was answered.

What does it read to show my AI sessions?

Only what Claude Code, Codex and the ChatGPT desktop app already keep on your Mac. From the start: the names, dates and sizes of Claude Code and Codex transcript files, and the update times in the ChatGPT desktop app’s local chat list. What is inside those files, chat titles and the Claude app’s usage file are read only if you switch those on. Token counts are kept in memory unless you opt in to saving a history file, which you can delete at any time. Their daily totals by model are part of the usage data Oriel sends.

Why would it ask for an administrator password?

Only for the optional fan-control and lid-closed helper, once, from Settings. It’s a separate program with a pinned connection to the app, and it hands the fans back to macOS if the app quits, crashes or anything looks hot.

Will macOS warn me when I open it?

Today, yes: current builds aren’t notarized by Apple, so macOS asks for approval in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security. The plan is to notarize before the official download opens.

Is it open source?

No, not the app. Oriel is the new name for the app released until now as LocalNook; from LocalNook 0.5.0 on it is proprietary software, and LocalNook releases 0.2.0 to 0.4.8 stay under the GPL for the people who have them. The root fan helper, a separate program, is GPL-3.0-or-later, and its complete source is public at Kkray19/LocalNook-fan-helper-source. Full notices are in the app under About ▸ License.

Does it cost anything?

No. Oriel is free: no price, no subscription, no account and no licence key.

How do I get help?

A proper support route is one of the open launch items. Questions about privacy and the License Agreement are answered at oriel.nook@gmail.com. See the support page for where things stand.
